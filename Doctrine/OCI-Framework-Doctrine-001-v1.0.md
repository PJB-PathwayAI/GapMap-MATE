# Operational Capability Intelligence (OCI)
## A Framework for Understanding Human Capability
### Doctrine 001 — Issue 1.0

> Qualifications describe what someone has achieved.
> Capability describes what someone may yet achieve.
> Operational Capability Intelligence begins by recognising the difference.

---

## Purpose

Operational Capability Intelligence (OCI) is a framework for understanding human capability.

It has been developed through lived experience, military service, engineering, workforce development and practical application across education and industry.

OCI is not presented as a replacement for qualifications, competency frameworks or existing educational systems. Rather, it complements them by providing a structured way to understand capability alongside knowledge, experience and achievement.

This document is offered as a contribution to the national conversation on education, technical pathways, workforce development and lifelong learning.

Its purpose is simple:

To encourage better decisions by improving how we recognise, understand and develop human capability.

## Foreword

I did not set out to create a workforce framework.

I set out to change my own life.

At twenty-one years old I found myself sitting in a classroom learning fractions. Not because I wanted to become a mathematician, but because I wanted the opportunity to transfer into the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (REME).

I wasn't naturally academic.

School had never convinced me that education was where I would find my future.

But somebody looked beyond my qualifications.

They saw someone willing to learn.

They saw someone capable of developing.

That decision changed the direction of my life.

Over the following twenty-three years the Army taught me far more than engineering.

It taught me that capability is rarely created by a single qualification.

It is developed through experience.

Responsibility.

Reflection.

Mentorship.

Opportunity.

Confidence.

Purpose.

Every role built upon the last.

Every challenge revealed another gap.

Every lesson created another opportunity.

When I eventually left military service, I expected the civilian world to recognise capability in much the same way.

Instead, I found organisations measuring qualifications, job titles and years of experience whilst still struggling to answer one deceptively simple question.

What is this person actually capable of?

That question has followed me ever since.

Operational Capability Intelligence was born from trying to answer it.

Compliance.

Experience.

Qualifications matter.

They represent knowledge gained.

Standards met.

Commitment demonstrated.

Achievement earned.

They should continue to do so.

But qualifications are not capability.

Operational Capability Intelligence does not diminish qualifications.

Knowledge.

Skills.

Experience.

Judgement.

Adaptability.

Motivation.

Purpose.

Opportunity.

## The Hidden Capability Problem

Every community contains people whose capability is not immediately visible.

The apprentice who struggles academically but thrives in practical environments.

The service leaver whose leadership has never been described in civilian language.

The parent returning to work after years of raising a family.

The engineer changing careers.

The young person who appears disengaged, not because they lack ability, but because nobody has yet recognised how they learn best.

These individuals are not lacking capability.

Often, they are lacking recognition.

Capability exists before it is recognised.

The question is whether our systems are designed to see it.

## Why This Matters Now

The United Kingdom is entering a period of significant change.

Government is strengthening technical education pathways and increasing employer engagement from an earlier age.

Organisations are adapting to artificial intelligence, automation and changing workforce demands.

People will retrain multiple times throughout their working lives.

At the same time, too many capable people continue to become disconnected from education, employment or training — not because they lack potential, but because existing systems struggle to recognise capability before opportunity is lost.

This presents an opportunity.

Not simply to improve how we measure achievement.

But to improve how we understand capability.

Operational Capability Intelligence is a response to that opportunity.

## Introducing Operational Capability Intelligence

Operational Capability Intelligence is the structured understanding of human capability to support better decisions about learning, development, readiness and opportunity.

OCI begins from one simple belief.

Capability exists before it is recognised.

OCI exists to reduce the distance between the two.

It does not attempt to predict who somebody will become.

It reduces uncertainty by providing a richer understanding of the capability they possess today, how that capability has developed, and where that capability could reasonably lead with the right opportunity, support and experience.

---

## The Three Principles

### Situational Awareness

Capability must first become visible.

Situational Awareness is the process of recognising capability that already exists, even when it is not immediately reflected through qualifications or traditional measures.

A teacher may observe exceptional practical problem-solving in a pupil who struggles with written examinations.

A military commander may recognise leadership potential before formal promotion.

An employer may identify transferable capability rather than simply previous job titles.

Better decisions begin with better understanding.

### Operational Readiness

Understanding capability allows individuals and organisations to make better-informed decisions.

Operational Readiness considers whether existing capability aligns with the demands of a role, a task or a future opportunity.

It asks not simply whether someone is qualified, but whether they are prepared, supported and ready to succeed.

Readiness is evidence-informed rather than assumption-led.

### Workforce Capability

Capability is never static.

It develops throughout life.

Workforce Capability focuses on developing existing capability whilst reducing uncertainty about where that capability could reasonably lead next.

It recognises that learning, experience and opportunity continue to shape capability long after formal education has finished.

---

## What OCI Is Not

Operational Capability Intelligence is not:

- A replacement for qualifications.
- An artificial intelligence scoring system.
- A recruitment methodology.
- A personality assessment.
- A competency framework.
- A performance management process.

OCI complements existing systems by providing an additional perspective through which capability can be understood.

---

## From Philosophy to Practice

Operational Capability Intelligence is intended to be applied wherever better understanding of human capability improves decision-making.

At an organisational level, it supports workforce visibility and strategic capability planning.

At an individual level, it supports self-understanding, career development and informed decision-making.

During transition, it supports people in recognising how existing capability can be applied to future opportunities.

Current applications of the framework include:

- **InsightCP** – applying OCI to organisational workforce capability and situational awareness.
- **GapMap** – applying OCI to individual capability discovery and development.
- **GapMap MATE** – applying OCI to transition readiness and future opportunity.

The technology is not the framework.

It is one practical expression of it.
